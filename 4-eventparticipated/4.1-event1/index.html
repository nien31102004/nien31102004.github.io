Event 1 - Seminar on Professional Knowledge and Personal Development Skills

Reflection Report on the Professional Knowledge and Personal Development Seminar

General Introduction

During the sixth week of the internship program, I attended a student seminar combining technology knowledge, learning experience, soft skills, and real-world product-development stories.

Unlike the weeks mainly focused on AWS labs, this seminar allowed me to learn from people who had participated in training programs, technology competitions, and practical projects. The sessions helped me identify not only technical knowledge but also the personal and communication skills required in the information-technology industry.

Event Objectives

  • Learn more about AWS learning and practice platforms.
  • Understand Hackathon preparation and teamwork under time constraints.
  • Recognize the importance of confidence in communication, study, and work.
  • Explore the lifecycle of a real product from idea to operation.
  • Understand procrastination and methods for improving personal productivity.
  • Observe effective presentation and audience-engagement techniques.

1. AWS Cloud Quest and AWS Learning Platforms

The first session introduced AWS Cloud Quest and other learning resources in the AWS ecosystem. Instead of only reading theory, AWS Cloud Quest provides an interactive environment where learners solve tasks based on simulated real-world situations.

I learned that effective cloud learning should combine official documentation, hands-on labs, and personal notes or projects. The speaker also recommended starting with core services such as IAM, EC2, VPC, S3, and RDS before moving into Serverless, Data Analytics, or AI/ML.

The session showed me that learning many services without practice does not create strong technical skills. A better approach is to learn by service group, complete a lab, verify the result, and remove unused resources afterward.

Key Lessons

  • Learning objectives should be small and measurable.
  • Labs convert theoretical knowledge into practical experience.
  • AWS certifications become more valuable when supported by projects.
  • Recording errors and solutions saves time when similar issues appear again.

2. Hackathon Experience

The second session was delivered by students with Hackathon experience. Their presentation was conducted in English and demonstrated strong preparation, communication, and professional delivery.

The speakers described a Hackathon as an environment where teams solve a problem within a limited period. The challenge involves not only coding but also idea selection, scope management, task allocation, integration, and presentation.

One important lesson was that a team should not attempt to build too many features. A successful Hackathon product should focus on a clear problem, demonstrate a complete workflow, and communicate its value to users.

The speakers emphasized:

  • Analyzing the challenge and identifying the main user need.
  • Assigning work according to team members’ strengths.
  • Agreeing on technologies early.
  • Integrating modules continuously.
  • Preparing demo data and a backup plan.
  • Presenting the problem, solution, and outcome as a clear story.

Connection to My Group Project

These lessons were directly relevant to the Secure AI-Driven Document Platform. Our team divided responsibilities into Frontend, Authentication & API, Backend, Security, AI, and Infrastructure.

I understood that agreeing on architecture, API contracts, and naming conventions early is essential. When each member develops separately without regular integration checks, the team may encounter major problems near the deadline.

3. Confidence Development

The third session focused on confidence in communication, learning, and public speaking. The speaker explained that confidence is not a fixed trait. It can be improved through preparation, practice, and experience.

Many students hesitate because they fear giving a wrong answer or being judged. However, avoiding communication makes improvement difficult.

Useful methods included:

  • Preparing content carefully.
  • Dividing a presentation into short and memorable sections.
  • Practicing and recording one’s voice.
  • Focusing on the message instead of small mistakes.
  • Asking questions when something is unclear.
  • Joining team activities to gain communication experience.

Personal Application

This session helped me become more confident when discussing Cognito, JWT, API Gateway, and access control with my team. Instead of keeping technical uncertainty to myself, I learned to raise questions and verify the solution together.

This skill was also useful when preparing the project demonstration because the presenter needed to explain the system workflow clearly rather than only list AWS services.

4. The Tử Vi Đại Việt Website Project

The fourth session introduced a real website project and its development journey. The speaker discussed the initial idea, feature development, deployment, operation, and promotion.

The most important lesson was that a product does not end when coding is completed. After launch, the developer must handle bugs, improve user experience, monitor performance, and update content.

The product-development process included:

  1. Identifying target users.
  2. Defining the problem or need.
  3. Building an initial version with core features.
  4. Releasing the product to users.
  5. Collecting feedback.
  6. Fixing issues and improving the product.
  7. Maintaining and promoting the service.

Relevance to the Cloud Project

Our group project also needed to be treated as a complete product. It was not enough for individual Lambda functions to work. The sign-in flow, upload interface, status messages, access control, and user understanding of inspection results were equally important.

I realized that returning technically correct JSON was not sufficient. APIs also needed clear responses, appropriate status codes, and documentation for Frontend integration.

5. Understanding Procrastination

The final session focused on procrastination. The speaker explained that procrastination is not always caused by laziness. It may come from pressure, fear of poor results, unclear goals, or tasks that appear too large.

Recommended methods included:

  • Breaking large tasks into smaller units.
  • Assigning a deadline to each unit.
  • Prioritizing important work.
  • Starting with a simple action.
  • Reducing distractions.
  • Tracking progress daily.
  • Maintaining consistent work habits.

Application During the Internship

For AWS Workshops and the group project, broad tasks such as “complete Authentication” were difficult to start. I therefore divided the work into smaller steps: creating a Cognito User Pool, configuring an App Client, enabling Managed Login, creating /me, building an HTTP API, adding a JWT Authorizer, testing tokens, defining scopes, creating groups, and validating User/Admin access.

This approach improved progress tracking and made troubleshooting easier.

Personal Event Experience

The seminar had a friendly atmosphere and covered a wide range of subjects. I learned not only from the technical content but also from the way speakers organized ideas, used examples, and interacted with the audience.

Outcomes and Lessons Learned

After the event, I:

  • Learned about additional AWS learning platforms.
  • Understood Hackathon preparation and teamwork.
  • Recognized the importance of communication and confidence.
  • Gained a more realistic view of product development.
  • Applied task decomposition to reduce procrastination.
  • Improved my approach to project documentation and demonstration.
  • Understood that technical and soft skills must be developed together.

Conclusion

The seminar supported both my internship learning and personal development. Its lessons on confidence, teamwork, time management, and product thinking were directly applied to the Authentication & API module of my group project.

The event helped me understand that becoming a capable cloud engineer or developer requires not only technical implementation, but also communication, collaboration, and continuous learning.
